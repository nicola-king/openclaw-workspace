2. Key Demand Drivers

(cid:127) Saudi Vision 2030 Mega-Projects — NEOM ($500B planned), Red Sea Project, Qiddiya entertainment city,
ROSHN housing, Diriyah Gate. All prioritize rapid, cost-effective construction.

(cid:127) Severe Housing Shortage — Saudi Arabia needs 1.5 million new homes by 2030. The Eskan program alone
targets 500,000 affordable housing units. Kuwait building permits dropped 46% in 2020 — massive catch-up demand.

(cid:127) Cost Advantage — Steel prefab saves up to 60% vs traditional construction. In a labor-scarce, high-heat
environment, factory fabrication wins.

(cid:127) Speed — Folding houses install in 3-5 minutes. Traditional methods take months. Critical for worker camps, disaster
relief, and urgent housing.

(cid:127) Post-War Reconstruction — Gaza (80%+ housing destroyed), Syria, Yemen. UN agencies actively procuring
folding container houses for temporary and transitional shelter.

(cid:127) Labor Camps — Massive migrant workforce (millions in Saudi/UAE alone) requires rapid deployment of
accommodation. Bulk procurement, standardized units.

(cid:127) Disaster Relief — 2023 Turkey-Syria earthquake triggered massive folding house exports from China. Middle East
remains earthquake/conflict-prone.

(cid:127) Tourism Growth — Saudi Red Sea luxury tourism, UAE desert glamping — unique demand for high-end prefab
structures.

5. Competitive Landscape

The Saudi prefab market is moderately concentrated. Chinese companies compete primarily on price and delivery speed.
Local players dominate government relationships.

Local Manufacturers: Red Sea Housing, Zamil, Al-Shahin, Kirby — strong government ties, established supply chains,
but limited folding-house technology.

Chinese Exporters: Moneybox Steel Structure, Junan Steel — competitive pricing (30-50% below local), folding house
expertise, but weaker local presence.

Western / Korean JV: Samsung C&T; + PIF, Mighty Buildings — technology leaders, high cost, focus on
premium/eco-segment.

6. Market Entry Strategy

(cid:127) Phase 1 — Digital Lead Generation: Alibaba International (targeted Saudi/UAE RFQs), LinkedIn outreach to
procurement managers, B2B directories (go4worldbusiness, TradeKey).

(cid:127) Phase 2 — Distributor Partnership: Sign exclusive distribution with Speed House (UAE) or Al-Jazira (KSA).
Revenue-share model. Leverage their government relationships.

(cid:127) Phase 3 — Government Tenders: Register in Saudi Etimad/Etimad Plus procurement platform. Target Eskan
housing program, Red Sea Development Company tenders.

(cid:127) Phase 4 — Local Presence: Representative office in Dubai (free zone) or Riyadh. Attend Big 5 Saudi (annual), BDE
UAE (annual).

(cid:127) Phase 5 — Local Assembly: Partner with Zamil or Al-Shahin for local assembly/CKD model. Bypass 15-25% import
duties.
